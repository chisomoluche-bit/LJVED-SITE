About LJVDE Farms

A registered Nigerian agribusiness growing, and in time processing, palm oil to standards that stand up to scrutiny.

Our story

Nigeria was once the world's leading palm oil supplier and today imports a large share of what it consumes. LJVDE Farms Limited was formed to be part of the recovery: building a well-run, well-documented oil palm estate in the region where the crop has always thrived.

We are an early-stage company and we say so. Our first priority is to establish a high-quality plantation, then to add our own mill, and to do both in a way that can be verified.

LJVDE Farms Limited is registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission of Nigeria.

What guides us

Integrity and openness

We keep accurate records, state our targets as targets, and are candid about risks.

Stewardship of land

We plant on land already in agricultural use and plan for soil, water and long-term productivity.

Quality

Good planting material, good agronomy and fresh-fruit processing produce better oil.

Long-term thinking

Oil palm is a twenty-five-year crop. We make decisions with that horizon in mind.

Where we work

Our estate is in Egbeda Village, Odeda Local Government Area, Ogun State, within Nigeria's traditional oil palm belt. The region combines suitable rainfall and soils with road access to major markets in the south-west.

How we are run

We operate with documented financial controls and expenditure tracked against approved budgets. Reporting to our stakeholders is structured and regular.

Four stages, one business

Land and planting

Documented, geolocated land. High-yielding Tenera planting material from the Nigerian Institute for Oil Palm Research (NIFOR).

Plantation care

Agronomy planned around soil, water and yield, with records kept to certification standards.

Owned processing

A mill built to international standards, so fruit is processed fresh and oil quality is controlled.

Market

Nigerian demand first, with the standards in place to serve regulated export markets over time.

From one fruit, three products

Once our mill is operating, palm fruit is processed into:

Crude palm oil

Pressed from the fleshy fruit. Used in cooking, food manufacturing and soap.

Palm kernel oil

Extracted from the kernel. Used in cosmetics, confectionery and cleaning products.

Palm kernel cake

The residue after kernel oil extraction, used as livestock feed.

The life of an oil palm

Oil palm takes a few years to begin fruiting, then produces for around twenty-five years.

Our plan

We grow in stages, proving each before committing to the next.

Phase 1 · Foundation

Establish

  • Phase 1 land secured and in preparation
  • Planting from the 2027 season
  • Compliance and reporting systems built in from the start
Phase 2 · Next five years

Scale to 100 acres with our own mill

  • Additional land, taking the estate toward 100 acres
  • Planting and plantation management through establishment

Phase 3 · Longer term

Expand

Beyond year five: a larger estate and commercial-scale milling, potentially alongside outgrower partnerships. This is direction, not a commitment.

Why the mill comes early

Fresh fruit matters

Palm fruit must be processed soon after harvest. A mill close to the estate protects oil quality.

Traceability stays in one place

Plot-to-mill records are what certification schemes and export buyers ask for.

Growth of this kind is supported by patient capital. We describe how we work with partners on our partnerships page.

Sustainability

Customers, banks and regulators increasingly ask how palm oil was grown. We are building to be able to answer.

RSPO certification pathway

Our membership application is under way, with certification as the 2030 target. Timing depends on the RSPO process.

Traceable land records

Surveyed boundaries converted to geospatial data, so every plot can be located and its land history documented. This is also the core requirement of the EU deforestation regulation.

No new deforestation

Planting is on land already in agricultural use. We do not clear primary forest.

Soil, water and inputs

Site planning for drainage and irrigation, and measured use of fertiliser and crop protection.

Local jobs and incomes

Permanent and seasonal employment, and local procurement wherever possible.

Import substitution

Every tonne produced locally reduces reliance on imported oil and the foreign exchange it consumes.

Contribution to the UN Sustainable Development Goals

Our activities contribute to zero hunger (Goal 2), decent work and economic growth (Goal 8), responsible consumption and production (Goal 12) and life on land (Goal 15).
